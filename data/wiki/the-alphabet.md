The Bulgarian alphabet, which uses the Cyrillic script, consists of 30 letters. It has its roots in the Glagolitic script developed by Saints Cyril and Methodius in the 9th century. Bulgaria adopted the Glagolitic alphabet in 886, but it was gradually replaced by the Cyrillic script, created in the Preslav Literary School in the early 10th century.

The Bulgarian alphabet includes printed and cursive characters, with distinct forms for handwritten versus standard printed text.
