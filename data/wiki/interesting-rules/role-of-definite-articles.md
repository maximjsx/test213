In Bulgarian, "the" is not a separate word. It is added to the end of the word.

| Gender | Ending | Example |
|----|----|----|
| Masculine | -ът / -а | градът, града (the city) |
| Masculine | -ят / -я | лекарят, лекаря (the doctor) |
| Feminine | -та | жената (the woman) |
| Neuter | -то | детето (the child) |
| Plural | -те / -та | градовете (the cities), децата (the children) |

### Masculine: full and short article

Masculine nouns have two forms: the full article (-ът, -ят) and the short article (-а, -я).

* градът (the city)
* входът (the entrance)

To choose between "градът" and "града", use this rule: if the noun is the subject of the sentence, use the full article, "-ът" (градът е голям, the city is big). Otherwise use the short one, "-а" (виждам града, I see the city).

Some masculine nouns take "-ят" and "-я" instead. Many of them are masculine professions ending in -ар or -яр:

* ==Лекарят== ми изписа рецепта. (The doctor wrote me a prescription.)
* Записах си час при ==лекаря==. (I booked an appointment with the doctor.)

In writing, using the correct article is essential.

**However**, in speaking, you can just say "==градъ==" instead of worrying about "градът" or "града".

### Adjective and noun

When an adjective comes before the noun, only the adjective gets the article:

* ==Хубавият== апартамент беше пребоядисан. (The nice apartment was repainted.)

With two adjectives, only the first one gets it:

* Окосихме ==дългата== зелена трева. (We mowed the long green grass.)

### Two adjectives joined by "и"

This one is tricky, and not even all Bulgarians know it:

* Българските и немски отбори се съревноваваха. The article is only on the first adjective, so these are mixed teams, each made of Bulgarians and Germans.
* Българските и немските отбори се съревноваваха. Both adjectives have the article, so the Bulgarian teams and the German teams are separate.
